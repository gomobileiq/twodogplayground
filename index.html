A Barely Viable Production

Personalized
AI Marketing

What happens when every prospect gets a personalized response to everything that matters to them — not a campaign pulled from a hat?

Traditional marketing automation starts with email autoresponders: canned messages fired based on open rates and clicks. Over time, branches, CRM triggers, and sales-rep handoffs might make it more sophisticated, but the content is still written in advance for a generic buyer.

Personalized AI marketing takes three leaps forward:

1

Identify Buyer

AI profiles each prospect using demographics, browsing behavior, social media, and prior interactions — building a picture of who they are before the first message is sent.

2

Leverage AI

Natural language understanding replaces click-tracking. The system reads what a prospect asks and responds in kind — across email, text, or phone.

3

Deliver Content

Content is created on the fly — coupons, upsells, comparison guides, reviews, product safety specs and buyer submitted videos — tailored to this person, right now.

The Product in This Demo

The Nina Ottosson by Outward Hound Hide-N-Slide is a Level 2 interactive dog puzzle toy. Dogs use their nose and paws to slide compartments and lift bone-shaped covers, revealing hidden treats. It encourages focus and problem-solving — ideal mental stimulation for young, energetic dogs.

Persona

Jake & Mia Chen

Early 30s · Dual-income couple · Chicago suburbs · Share one email address

Who They Are

Jake is a UX designer; Mia works in marketing analytics. They adopted Pickles — a four-month-old Labrador mix — six weeks ago from a shelter. Pickles is unambiguously their child. Their social feeds are already 40% dog photos.

What They Believe
  • Mental stimulation prevents the separation anxiety they've read about obsessively.
  • Their dog is exceptionally smart. (It's true. But they'd believe it regardless.)
  • Spending money on enrichment is not optional — it's responsible pet ownership.
Their Core Worry

They're both gone 7 a.m.–6 p.m. They worry about Pickles being bored, lonely, and destructive. They also worry — and will say so directly — that a Level 2 puzzle might be too easy for her after the first week.

How They Shop

They research obsessively before buying. They read reviews, watch YouTube, join subreddits, and ask questions. They expect brands to meet them where they are — informed, specific, and a little impatient with generic answers.

How Much Will They Spend

The Hide-N-Slide is $18. That's not a decision — it's a rounding error. Jake and Mia will spend whatever it takes to keep Pickles mentally stimulated, and they'll feel good about it. They're already budgeting for obedience classes, monthly subscription boxes, and a second puzzle toy before the first one arrives. A Level 3 upgrade, a snuffle mat, a slow feeder, a camera to watch her during the day — all inevitable. The real question isn't whether they'll spend. It's how fast you can get in front of them before a competitor does.

Discussion

Open discussion for roundtable participants. There are no right answers — the goal is to surface your reactions, questions, and instincts about where this is going.

  1. What do you think about the entire concept? First impressions — does this feel like a natural next step in marketing, or something genuinely new? What's your gut telling you?
  2. Would you hide the ball about using AI? Sarah is the only real person in this entire conversation. Every email and text before her call was AI. Should Two Dog Playground come clean about that — or does keeping it human-feeling actually serve the customer better? Helpful, or a little shady?
  3. Where does this feel helpful vs. creepy? The AI figured out Pickles was a Lab mix and ran with it — breed-specific content, unprompted. At what point does "hey, we noticed" turn into "wait, how do you know that?"
  4. Who owns the relationship? Sarah got the call — but only because the AI put it on her calendar. Is she in charge, or is the AI? Does it matter, as long as the customer's happy?
  5. What happens to the brand voice? If every customer gets a slightly different version of your brand, do you still have a brand? Or does "consistency" mean something different now?
  6. What's the economics? You used to pay copywriters to write emails a lot of people ignored. Now you pay for AI that writes emails people actually respond to. Is that obviously better — or are there hidden costs worth worrying about?
  7. How does trust get built? Jake and Mia casually mentioned Pickles solved the puzzle in 20 minutes. The AI turned that into a sales signal. What makes people comfortable sharing stuff like that — and what makes them feel played?
  8. Where would this break in your business? Where would this go sideways for you specifically? What's the customer conversation your AI would absolutely butcher?
  9. What's the first step? If you wanted to try even a tiny version of this in your own business — like, starting Monday — what would it be?
Facilitation Notes
  • No slides, no pitch — this is a conversation
  • All feedback is useful, including skepticism
  • Feel free to bring your own business context
  • Questions don't need answers — they need better questions
